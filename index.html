Leg 01About 7 minutes

Clear the cabin

Start with whatever is occupying your attention. This is not a test of insight. It is simply a way to make the noise visible before choosing what deserves the flight.

Tasks, worries, fragments, decisions and feelings all belong here. No order is needed.
What the research can and cannot say

Short writing exercises can help some people organise thoughts, but average psychological effects are generally small and vary by person and protocol. Treat this as attention clearing, not treatment.

Leg 02About 13 minutes

Follow one signal

Choose one question that matters now. The ladder will not reveal a hidden final truth. It helps you form a working explanation that you can compare with real experience.

Stop when the answer becomes repetitive, vague or invented. More rungs do not necessarily mean more truth.

Why this is framed as a hypothesis

People can build convincing explanations without having direct access to every cause of their own behaviour. Evidence and alternative explanations make the exercise more useful than repeated introspection alone.

Leg 03About 10 minutes

Check the conditions

A widely studied motivation framework focuses on autonomy, competence and relatedness. Satisfaction and frustration are different experiences, so this reflection checks both. It is not a validated score.

My recent choices have felt like my own.

Autonomy satisfaction

I have felt pressured into choices I do not endorse.

Autonomy frustration

I have felt capable and effective in things that matter.

Competence satisfaction

I have felt ineffective or unable to make progress.

Competence frustration

I have felt close to and understood by people who matter.

Relatedness satisfaction

I have felt excluded, lonely or emotionally distant.

Relatedness frustration
Evidence note

Self Determination Theory distinguishes satisfaction from active frustration of autonomy, competence and relatedness. These six prompts are a brief reflection inspired by that framework, not the validated BPNSFS questionnaire.

Leg 04About 13 minutes

Read one difficult signal

You do not need to process every difficult feeling at once. Choose one path that feels useful today. Each path offers possibilities, not a diagnosis.

Which explanations might be worth testing?
Evidence note

Envy can involve self improvement or hostility, depending on how the comparison is understood. Guilt often focuses attention on a behaviour and can support repair. The strong reaction path is deliberately presented as hypothesis generation because irritation does not prove projection.

Leg 05About 10 minutes

Draw one boundary

A boundary describes what you will communicate or do. It is not a rule that controls another person. Keep this specific enough that you would recognise it in real life.

Why this is separate from discipline

Planning your own response can reduce ambiguity. Relationship boundaries and personal habit formation are different problems, so the behaviour experiment comes next.

Leg 06About 17 minutes

Design a seven day experiment

Do not promise to become a different person. Choose one small behaviour that tests the insight you formed. The cue and response should both be observable.

If then planning can help translate an existing goal into behaviour. It works best when the goal already matters to the person, which is why this step asks what is worth acting on before creating the cue.
